Original prompt
A single-screen nautical tide instrument for an invented harbor. The viewer should feel they have opened a folded, slightly foxed survey sheet: warm paper #F3EBD8, deep ink #17333A, brass #B08A44. Draw several hairline waterlines at historical heights, a brass vertical gauge on the right, and a live water polygon whose surface is a sum of two sine tides (roughly 12h25 and 24h cycles). Interaction: hover any historical waterline to highlight its year and height; drag anywhere on the chart to scrub; keyboard arrows scrub by fifteen minutes; the live button restores the clock. Signature moment: the waterline visibly breathes with the real clock while a brass marker ticks upward, and scrubbing makes the entire harbor cross-section tilt fractionally like a floating ledger. Copy: specific harbor names, tide heights in metres, times in 24-hour notation, and margin notes in the voice of a harbourmaster. No generic instructions; label the drag affordance as a printed scale reading “DRAG THE HOUR”.
