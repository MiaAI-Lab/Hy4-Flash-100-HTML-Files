TITLE
The Cartographer’s Mistake — A Map That Resents Correction

PROMPT
A newsprint #E7E0CE chart table with a single hand-drawn coastline in ink #1C2A33 and one red #C43F2F correction pen. The map claims to show “THE COAST OF NORTH, SURVEYED 1886”. Along the bottom, a caption reads “The coastline has been wrong since Tuesday. Please correct it.” Dragging the red pen across the coast pushes the line away from the pointer with a soft spring; nearby contour lines follow, but slightly late. Each time the coast is corrected, one of five place names changes to a more honest invented name: HARBOUR OF ALMOST, CAPE NOON, THE SEA OF NEARLY, LOW TIDE MUSEUM, POINT OF NO RETURN TODAY. A counter records CORRECTIONS 00. Every fifth correction the map gives up and redraws the whole coast as a straight line, then slowly springs back into its wrong shape. Entrance: the chart unrolls from the top, the coastline draws itself in one gesture, and the pen drops into place. Signature moment: the coastline remembers every correction as a small ink scar that fades after a few seconds. No emoji, no glass, no purple. Reduced motion moves the coast instantly without scars.

DESCRIPTION
A map that resists accuracy. Correcting the coastline only makes it more elaborate, and the map keeps a temporary record of every edit.

VISUAL TECHNIQUES
Canvas spring chain coastline, pointer-driven displacement with delayed follower contours, live label substitutions, fading ink scars, unroll entrance.

INTERACTION MODEL
Drag the pen across the coast. The map records corrections when the coastline is displaced beyond a threshold. Keyboard: arrow keys move the pen.
