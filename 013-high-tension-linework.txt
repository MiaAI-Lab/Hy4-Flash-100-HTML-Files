TITLE
High-Tension Linework — A Surveyor's Panorama

PROMPT
A wide panoramic survey drawing, not a landscape photo. Ground is warm white #F4F2EB; ink is deep navy #142B3D with a single signal red #C7472B. Five overlapping mountain outlines occupy the middle of the page in flat vector shapes, each drawn with a different density of hatch marks (CSS repeating-linear-gradient or inline SVG patterns). At the top: small registration crosses, a scale bar, and the title “HIGH-TENSION LINEWORK” in spaced sans-serif capitals across one line. Beneath the ridges: five numbered peaks with exact invented names, elevations and one-sentence descriptions. Moving the pointer across the panorama moves a surveyor's vertical crosshair and updates a live readout of bearing and distance; the nearest peak's outline thickens and its hatch direction flips. Clicking a peak locks the readout and opens a short note at the bottom in a red-ruled margin. Signature moment: on load, the ridges draw themselves left to right with a single continuous pen stroke, then the hatch fills appear behind them. Copy must read like a field survey: “Kestrel Shoulder, 1,841 m — the last reliable cairn before the snow line”. No gradients on text, no cards, no icon fonts. Reduced motion shows the finished drawing.

DESCRIPTION
An interactive survey panorama in the language of old cartography. Pointer movement becomes a bearing measurement; peaks respond with changes to their drawn texture and supply precise, invented field notes.

VISUAL TECHNIQUES
Inline SVG polylines and pattern fills, SVG stroke-dasharray drawing animation, pointer-driven crosshair transform, nearest-point calculation, live aria-locked readout, exact two-colour plus red palette.

INTERACTION MODEL
Move across the drawing to measure; click a peak to lock a note; use peak buttons for keyboard access. All readouts update live.
